Golden-Crested Myna

  • Common Names: Golden-crested myna, golden-crested mynah, Ampeliceps coronatus.

  • Status: Least Concern, though habitat loss can affect local populations.

  • Range: Northeastern India and parts of Southeast Asia, including Myanmar, southern China, Thailand, Laos, Cambodia, and Vietnam.

  • Habitat: Moist lowland forests, bamboo groves, evergreen foothills, and forest edges.

  • Size & Weight: About 7.5-8.5 inches long; 2.7-3.5 oz (78-99 g).

  • Lifespan: Estimated at 15-20 years in managed care.

Physical Description

  • Males: Glossy black with a broad golden-yellow crest across the forehead, crown, nape, and throat.
  • Females: Mostly glossy black, with less golden plumage on the throat.

Predators & Conservation:

  • Predators: Arboreal snakes, raptors, and climbing mammals can prey on eggs or young birds.
  • Conservation: Logging, forest fragmentation, and capture for the songbird trade are concerns in parts of its range.

Diet

  • Wild: Fruit, berries, canopy insects, and occasionally nectar or small vertebrates.

  • Human Care: Low-iron softbill pellets with fruit, greens, and suitable insects.

Reproduction

  • Nesting: Monogamous pairs nest in tree cavities; clutches usually contain several eggs.

  • Parental Care: Both parents feed the chicks in the nest.

  • Development: Chicks hatch helpless and typically fledge at about three weeks.

Behavior & Key Traits

  • Key Trait: A starling relative that forages high in the forest canopy.

  • Behavior: Shows bright yellow patches on the wings in flight.

  • Adaptation: Produces varied calls, including whistles and clicks.

  • Ecological Role: May mimic sounds, as do other mynas.
