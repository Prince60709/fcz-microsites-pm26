Chinese Hwamei

  • Common Names: Chinese laughingthrush, melodious laughingthrush, Garrulax canorus.

  • Status: Least Concern globally, but trapping pressures some regional populations.

  • Range: Southern and central China, northern and central Vietnam, and Laos; introduced populations occur elsewhere in Asia.

  • Habitat: Thickets, bamboo, scrub, open woodland, secondary forest, and tall grass.

  • Size & Weight: About 8-10 inches long and 1.8-2.6 oz (50-74 g).

  • Lifespan: Up to about 10 years in the wild; often longer in managed care.

Physical Description

  • Males: Reddish-brown plumage with a bold white eye-ring and trailing stripe; males are renowned for their loud songs.
  • Females: Similar reddish-brown plumage and white eye-ring; females tend to use shorter, less varied calls.

Predators & Conservation:

  • Predators: Snakes and raptors may take adults, eggs, or young birds.
  • Conservation: Songbird trapping and habitat change are concerns; international trade is regulated under CITES Appendix II.

Diet

  • Wild: Ground insects such as ants, beetles, and caterpillars, plus berries, fruit, seeds, and grains.

  • Human Care: Softbill pellets, minced fruit, and live invertebrates such as crickets or mealworms.

Reproduction

  • Nesting: Pairs build a cup-shaped nest low in grass or shrubs; clutches commonly contain 2-5 eggs.

  • Parental Care: Both parents feed the chicks; incubation lasts about two weeks.

  • Development: Young hatch helpless and leave the nest after a short nestling period.

Behavior & Key Traits

  • Key Trait: Often forages on the ground in dense cover.

  • Behavior: Turns over leaf litter while searching for food.

  • Adaptation: Males are celebrated for complex, repeated songs and mimicry.

  • Ecological Role: Usually remains in the same region year-round.
