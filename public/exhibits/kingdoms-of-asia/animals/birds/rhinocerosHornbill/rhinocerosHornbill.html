Rhinoceros Hornbill

  • Common Names: Rhinoceros hornbill, Buceros rhinoceros.

  • Status: Vulnerable; habitat loss and hunting have reduced populations in parts of its range.

  • Range: The Malay Peninsula, Sumatra, Java, and Borneo.

  • Habitat: Large trees in lowland and hill rainforests, including swamp forest.

  • Size & Weight: About 31-37 inches long; adults may weigh roughly 5-6.5 lbs.

  • Lifespan: May live for several decades; up to about 35 years in managed care.

Physical Description

  • Males: Large black-and-white hornbill with a yellow bill and prominent casque; males often have red eyes.
  • Females: Similar in plumage, but usually has white eyes and a smaller casque.

Predators & Conservation:

  • Predators: Eggs and chicks are vulnerable to arboreal predators; adults are threatened primarily by people.
  • Conservation: Deforestation and hunting for the casque threaten the species; protecting mature forest and nesting trees is essential.

Diet

  • Wild: Mostly fruit, especially figs, along with insects, small reptiles, and other small animals.

  • Human Care: A fruit-heavy diet supplemented with appropriate protein and nutrients in managed care.

Reproduction

  • Nesting: The female seals herself inside a tree cavity to incubate a small clutch of eggs.

  • Parental Care: The male brings food to the sealed nest; the female and chicks emerge when the nest period is complete.

  • Development: Chicks grow in the cavity and are fed by both parents before fledging.

Behavior & Key Traits

  • Key Trait: The casque is hollow and sits atop its large curved bill.

  • Behavior: Pairs use tree cavities for nesting.

  • Adaptation: Important seed dispersers in rainforest ecosystems.

  • Ecological Role: Has loud calls that can carry through the forest.
