Crested Wood Partridge

  • Common Names: Roul-roul partridge, crested partridge, Rollulus rouloul.

  • Status: Vulnerable; populations are declining as forests are cleared and fragmented.

  • Range: Myanmar, southern Thailand, Malaysia, Sumatra, and Borneo.

  • Habitat: Humid evergreen lowland rainforest, especially dense forest-floor undergrowth.

  • Size & Weight: About 10-11 inches long and 6.5-8.5 oz (180-240 g).

  • Lifespan: Estimated at 10-15 years in managed environments.

Physical Description

  • Males: Metallic violet-blue with a white forehead patch and a tall, bristly red crest.
  • Females: Green-bodied with chestnut-brown wings and a gray head; lacks the male's large crest.

Predators & Conservation:

  • Predators: Snakes, raptors, and other forest predators may take eggs and young.
  • Conservation: Logging, agriculture, and forest fragmentation are driving declines in its Sundaic rainforest range.

Diet

  • Wild: Ants, termites, beetles, snails, worms, fallen fruit, berries, and seeds.

  • Human Care: A balanced ground-bird diet with seeds, greens, fruit, and appropriate insects.

Reproduction

  • Nesting: Monogamous pairs construct a covered nest on the ground; the female lays about 5-6 eggs.

  • Parental Care: The female incubates; both parents protect and feed the chicks.

  • Development: Eggs hatch after about 18 days; chicks forage with the adults.

Behavior & Key Traits

  • Key Trait: Usually walks or runs through forest undergrowth rather than flying.

  • Behavior: Uses camouflage to hide in leaf litter.

  • Adaptation: May roost in low trees at night.

  • Ecological Role: Its name 'Roul-roul' echoes its whistling call.
