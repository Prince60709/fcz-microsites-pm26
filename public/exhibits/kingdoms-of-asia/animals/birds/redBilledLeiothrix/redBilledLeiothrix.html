Red-Billed Leiothrix

  • Common Names: Red-billed leiothrix, Pekin robin, Pekin nightingale, Leiothrix lutea.

  • Status: Least Concern globally; introduced populations can affect local ecosystems.

  • Range: Native to the Himalayas, southern China, and Southeast Asia; introduced populations occur in places including Hawaii and Japan.

  • Habitat: Hill and pine forests, bamboo, and dense shrub understory.

  • Size & Weight: About 4-6 inches long and roughly 0.75 oz (17-20 g).

  • Lifespan: Often 8-15 years in managed environments.

Physical Description

  • Males: Olive-green with a bright red bill, yellow-orange throat, and vivid yellow, orange, and scarlet wing markings.
  • Females: Similar to males but generally duller; juveniles have dark bills.

Predators & Conservation:

  • Predators: Small raptors, snakes, and other nest predators.
  • Conservation: Trapping for the cage-bird trade is a concern; introduced populations are monitored for effects on native birds.

Diet

  • Wild: Insects and other invertebrates, berries, fruit, and seeds.

  • Human Care: Softbill pellets, fruit, and suitable live or prepared invertebrates.

Reproduction

  • Nesting: Pairs breed in spring and summer, building an open cup nest in shrubbery; clutches usually hold 2-5 eggs.

  • Parental Care: Both parents incubate and feed the chicks.

  • Development: Chicks fledge quickly, often around two weeks after hatching.

Behavior & Key Traits

  • Key Trait: A lively, social babbler that often forages in flocks.

  • Behavior: Males sing complex, melodious songs.

  • Adaptation: Often stays concealed in dense vegetation while calling loudly.

  • Ecological Role: Disperses seeds after eating fruit.
