Green-Naped Pheasant-Pigeon

  • Common Names: Pheasant-pigeon, noble pheasant-pigeon, Otidiphaps nobilis.

  • Status: Least Concern, although habitat loss is contributing to declines.

  • Range: Western New Guinea and nearby islands, including Batanta and Waigeo.

  • Habitat: Primary tropical rainforest, especially hilly and lower-mountain forest.

  • Size & Weight: About 15-20 inches long and approximately 1.1 lbs (500 g).

  • Lifespan: May live up to about 20 years under professional care.

Physical Description

  • Males: Copper or chestnut body with an iridescent blue-green patch on the nape, dark head and tail, and red bill.
  • Females: Similar to males in appearance; both sexes have the distinctive green nape and red bill.

Predators & Conservation:

  • Predators: Large snakes and raptors may prey on birds or eggs; forest loss is the principal threat.
  • Conservation: Depends on intact rainforest; logging and deforestation threaten its habitat.

Diet

  • Wild: Fallen fruit, berries, and seeds, with occasional small invertebrates.

  • Human Care: Fruit and seeds with other suitable pigeon foods in managed care.

Reproduction

  • Nesting: Faithful pairs build a simple nest on or near the ground and usually lay one egg.

  • Parental Care: Both parents incubate and feed the chick, including with crop milk.

  • Development: The chick is cared for by both parents until it can feed independently.

Behavior & Key Traits

  • Key Trait: A pigeon that resembles a pheasant and lives mainly on the forest floor.

  • Behavior: Often walks slowly and pumps its tail as it moves.

  • Adaptation: Flushes into nearby cover when startled.

  • Ecological Role: Helps disperse seeds by eating fruit.
