White-Throated Ground Dove

  • Common Names: White-fronted ground dove, Mariana ground dove, Pampusana xanthonura.

  • Status: Near Threatened; populations are decreasing due to habitat loss and introduced predators.

  • Range: Yap and the Northern Mariana Islands in Micronesia; formerly occurred on Guam.

  • Habitat: Native limestone and secondary forests, as well as moist tropical or subtropical woodland.

  • Size & Weight: About 10 inches long; roughly 2.1-5 oz (58.5-140 g).

  • Lifespan: Can live up to about 17 years in managed care.

Physical Description

  • Males: White head, neck, and breast contrast with a brown body and a wine-red or bronze band across the upper back.
  • Females: Mostly tawny-brown or bronzy-olive, without the male's white head and breast.

Predators & Conservation:

  • Predators: Introduced brown tree snakes devastated the Guam population; other snakes and raptors may prey on birds and eggs.
  • Conservation: Habitat loss and invasive predators threaten island populations; captive breeding and predator-free refuges support recovery.

Diet

  • Wild: Fruits, including figs, with seeds, flowers, and tender leaves.

  • Human Care: A fruit-rich pigeon diet with suitable seeds and greens.

Reproduction

  • Nesting: Pairs build a twig platform in trees or shrubs and generally lay one egg.

  • Parental Care: Both parents incubate and feed the chick with crop milk.

  • Development: The chick is raised in the nest until it can forage independently.

Behavior & Key Traits

  • Key Trait: Despite its name, some populations forage and nest above ground.

  • Behavior: Usually shy, secretive, and territorial.

  • Adaptation: Males and females have markedly different plumage.

  • Ecological Role: Disperses seeds across island forests.
