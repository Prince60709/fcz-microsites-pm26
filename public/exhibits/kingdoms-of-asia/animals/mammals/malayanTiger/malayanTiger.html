Malayan Tiger

  • Common Names: Malayan tiger, southern Indochinese tiger, Panthera tigris jacksoni.

  • Status: Critically Endangered; fewer than 150 mature individuals are estimated to remain in the wild.

  • Range: The Malay Peninsula, including Peninsular Malaysia and the southernmost part of Thailand.

  • Habitat: Tropical and subtropical rainforests, hill forests, and connected forest landscapes.

  • Size & Weight: Males: about 75-112 inches long and 104-285 lbs; females: about 70-94 inches and 52-195 lbs.

  • Lifespan: About 15-20 years in the wild; up to 20 years or more in human care.

Physical Description

  • Males: Larger than females, with a reddish-orange coat and individually unique black stripes.
  • Females: Smaller than males, with the same orange, black, and white pattern.

Predators & Conservation:

  • Predators: Adults have no regular natural predators; people are the principal threat.
  • Conservation: Poaching, prey depletion, and forest fragmentation threaten the species. Protected areas, anti-poaching patrols, habitat corridors, and managed breeding support recovery.

Diet

  • Wild: An obligate carnivore that hunts deer, wild boar, serow, and other medium-to-large mammals.

  • Human Care: A nutritionally balanced meat-based carnivore diet under veterinary supervision.

Reproduction

  • Nesting: Solitary adults meet to breed; after roughly 100 days of gestation, females give birth to a litter, commonly 2-5 cubs.

  • Parental Care: The mother raises the cubs alone, nursing them and later teaching them to hunt.

  • Development: Cubs stay with their mother for about two years before becoming independent.

Behavior & Key Traits

  • Key Trait: Usually solitary and territorial.

  • Behavior: Most active at dusk and at night, when it stalks prey.

  • Adaptation: Marks its range with scent and scratch marks.

  • Ecological Role: Can swim well and may cross waterways.
