Sumatran Orangutan

  • Common Names: Sumatran orangutan, red ape, Pongo abelii.

  • Status: Critically Endangered; only a small, declining population remains in the wild.

  • Range: Northern Sumatra, Indonesia, especially the Leuser Ecosystem and Aceh.

  • Habitat: Tropical rainforests, peat swamps, river valleys, and montane forests.

  • Size & Weight: Males: about 4.5-6 feet tall and 110-200 lbs; females: about 3-4 feet and 66-110 lbs.

  • Lifespan: About 35-45 years in the wild; up to 50-55 years in managed care.

Physical Description

  • Males: Adult males are larger and may develop broad cheek pads and a throat sac.
  • Females: Smaller than adult males and usually lack cheek pads.

Predators & Conservation:

  • Predators: Humans are the main threat; natural predators can include Sumatran tigers, clouded leopards, pythons, and crocodiles.
  • Conservation: Deforestation, fires, hunting, and illegal wildlife trade threaten the species. Protected forest corridors help connect isolated populations.

Diet

  • Wild: Mostly fruit, especially figs and durian, with leaves, bark, flowers, insects, and eggs also eaten.

  • Human Care: A varied plant-based diet centered on fruit and vegetables, supplemented with browse and other appropriate foods.

Reproduction

  • Nesting: Females give birth to a single infant after a long pregnancy, generally at intervals of about 8-9 years.

  • Parental Care: The mother provides prolonged care and teaches her infant essential foraging and climbing skills.

  • Development: Young orangutans may remain dependent on their mother for up to eight years.

Behavior & Key Traits

  • Key Trait: Spends most of its life in the forest canopy.

  • Behavior: Builds a fresh sleeping nest in a tree each night.

  • Adaptation: Uses sticks and other objects to obtain food.

  • Ecological Role: Generally lives alone, though fruiting trees can bring individuals together.
