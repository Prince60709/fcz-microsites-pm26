Malayan Tapir

  • Common Names: Malayan tapir, Asian tapir, Tapirus indicus.

  • Status: Endangered; fewer than 2,500 mature individuals are estimated to remain in the wild.

  • Range: Southern Myanmar and Thailand, Peninsular Malaysia, and Sumatra, Indonesia.

  • Habitat: Tropical rainforests, swamps, and lower montane forests, usually near water.

  • Size & Weight: About 6-8 feet long and 3-3.5 feet at the shoulder; 550-800 lbs.

  • Lifespan: About 25-30 years in the wild; up to 30-36 years in human care.

Physical Description

  • Males: Males and females look alike; females are often somewhat larger.
  • Females: Females may be larger than males; the distinctive black-and-white coat is shared by both sexes.

Predators & Conservation:

  • Predators: Tigers and leopards can prey on tapirs; people are the leading overall threat through habitat loss and roads.
  • Conservation: Deforestation, fragmentation, and road mortality threaten the species; forest corridors, wildlife crossings, and managed breeding are important.

Diet

  • Wild: Leaves, shoots, buds, twigs, fruit, and aquatic plants.

  • Human Care: A herbivore diet of browse, hay, vegetables, and fruit, adjusted to the animal's needs.

Reproduction

  • Nesting: Females give birth to a single calf after a gestation of about 13 months.

  • Parental Care: The mother nurses and protects the calf until it becomes more independent.

  • Development: Calves have reddish-brown coats with white stripes and spots; this juvenile pattern fades by about six months.

Behavior & Key Traits

  • Key Trait: The only tapir species native to Asia.

  • Behavior: Uses a flexible, grasping snout to gather vegetation.

  • Adaptation: An excellent swimmer that can submerge to evade danger.

  • Ecological Role: Has four toes on each front foot and three on each hind foot.
