Siamang

  • Common Names: Siamang, siamang gibbon, Symphalangus syndactylus.

  • Status: Endangered; populations have declined substantially and continue to decrease.

  • Range: The Malay Peninsula, Sumatra, and parts of southern Thailand.

  • Habitat: Tropical, mountain, and monsoon forests, mainly in the middle and upper canopy.

  • Size & Weight: About 29-35 inches tall; 22-31 lbs, with an arm span reaching about 59 inches.

  • Lifespan: About 25-30 years in the wild; 35-40 years or more in managed care.

Physical Description

  • Males: Males and females are similar in size and appearance, with long black fur and a large throat sac.
  • Females: Females are similar to males; adults can be distinguished more reliably by individual features than coat color.

Predators & Conservation:

  • Predators: Large snakes and raptors may take young animals; people are the primary threat through habitat loss and hunting.
  • Conservation: Forest clearance and the illegal pet trade are major threats; protected habitat and managed breeding help conserve the species.

Diet

  • Wild: Leaves and fruit, especially figs, supplemented with flowers, insects, eggs, and small vertebrates.

  • Human Care: A varied primate diet of leafy greens, vegetables, fruit, browse, and appropriate protein.

Reproduction

  • Nesting: Pairs are generally monogamous; after about seven months of gestation, females give birth to one infant.

  • Parental Care: The infant clings to its mother, and the father later takes an active role in carrying and caring for it.

  • Development: Young are gradually weaned and remain with their family group until maturity.

Behavior & Key Traits

  • Key Trait: Moves through trees by swinging hand-over-hand (brachiation).

  • Behavior: Pairs sing coordinated calls to defend territory and strengthen their bond.

  • Adaptation: An inflatable throat sac amplifies its loud calls.

  • Ecological Role: Walks upright on two legs when traveling on the ground.
