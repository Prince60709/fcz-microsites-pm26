Asian Small-Clawed River Otter

  • Common Names: Asian small-clawed otter, Oriental small-clawed otter, Asian short-clawed otter.

  • Status: Vulnerable; wild populations are decreasing.

  • Range: South and Southeast Asia, from southern India and southern China through mainland Southeast Asia to parts of Indonesia and the Philippines.

  • Habitat: Freshwater rivers, streams, lakes, marshes, mangroves, estuaries, rice paddies, and other shallow wetlands.

  • Size & Weight: About 2-3 feet total length; 4-11 lbs (2-5 kg).

  • Lifespan: About 8-10 years in the wild; up to 15-20 years in managed care.

Physical Description

  • Males: Males and females look alike; males are generally somewhat larger.
  • Females: Females are generally smaller, with the same dark coat and pale throat markings.

Predators & Conservation:

  • Predators: Pythons, crocodiles, and wild or domestic dogs; people are a major threat through habitat loss and trade.
  • Conservation: Threatened by wetland conversion, water pollution, prey loss, and illegal pet trade; zoo conservation programs support protected populations.

Diet

  • Wild: Crabs, mollusks, crayfish, aquatic insects, small fish, and amphibians.

  • Human Care: A balanced carnivore diet with fish, shellfish, and suitable insects.

Reproduction

  • Nesting: Pairs usually mate throughout the year; gestation is about 60-64 days, and litters contain 1-7 pups.

  • Parental Care: Pups are raised in family groups; older offspring may help care for later litters.

  • Development: Pups open their eyes at about a month and begin swimming at roughly two months.

Behavior & Key Traits

  • Key Trait: The smallest of the world's otter species.

  • Behavior: Uses dexterous front paws to feel for prey under rocks and in mud.

  • Adaptation: Highly social, living in family groups that may include 12-15 otters.

  • Ecological Role: Communicates with a varied repertoire of calls.
