Sloth Bear

  • Common Names: Sloth bear, honey bear, Indian bear, Melursus ursinus.

  • Status: Vulnerable; the wild population is decreasing.

  • Range: South Asia, particularly India, Sri Lanka, Nepal, and Bhutan.

  • Habitat: Dry and moist forests, grasslands, and rocky areas with caves or other shelter.

  • Size & Weight: About 5-6 feet long; females weigh about 120-210 lbs and males about 175-310 lbs.

  • Lifespan: Usually 15-20 years in the wild; up to about 40 years in captivity.

Physical Description

  • Males: Typically larger than females, with a shaggy black coat and pale chest marking.
  • Females: Smaller than males; otherwise similar in appearance, with long claws and a flexible snout.

Predators & Conservation:

  • Predators: Bengal tigers, leopards, and occasionally dholes may prey on bears, especially young animals.
  • Conservation: Habitat loss, poaching, and conflict with people are major threats; rescue and conflict-mitigation work supports wild bears.

Diet

  • Wild: Mostly termites and ants, supplemented with fruit, flowers, seeds, and honey.

  • Human Care: A varied omnivore diet with fruit, vegetables, and suitable protein sources.

Reproduction

  • Nesting: After a gestation of about 6-7 months, a female gives birth to 1-2 cubs, usually in a sheltered den.

  • Parental Care: The mother cares for the cubs and carries them on her back after they leave the den.

  • Development: Cubs emerge after several weeks and may remain with their mother for two to three years.

Behavior & Key Traits

  • Key Trait: Uses curved claws to open termite mounds.

  • Behavior: Can suck insects through its mobile lips and snout.

  • Adaptation: Usually forages at night or during cooler hours.

  • Ecological Role: Often carries cubs on its back.
