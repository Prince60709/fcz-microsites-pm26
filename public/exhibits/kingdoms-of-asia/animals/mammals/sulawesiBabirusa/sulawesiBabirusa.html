Sulawesi Babirusa

  • Common Names: North Sulawesi babirusa, pig-deer, Babyrousa celebensis.

  • Status: Vulnerable; the wild population is decreasing.

  • Range: Sulawesi and nearby Indonesian islands, including Lembeh, Buton, and Muna.

  • Habitat: Moist tropical forests, swampy areas, and forest near rivers, lakes, or salt licks.

  • Size & Weight: About 2-2.5 feet at the shoulder and 2.8-3.6 feet long; 130-220 lbs.

  • Lifespan: Up to about 10-11 years in the wild and 23-24 years in captivity.

Physical Description

  • Males: Males have prominent upper tusks that grow through the skin of the snout and curve upward.
  • Females: Females are generally smaller and have much smaller tusks; both sexes have sparse hair and gray-brown skin.

Predators & Conservation:

  • Predators: People and dogs are important threats; young animals may also be vulnerable to predators.
  • Conservation: Logging, agricultural expansion, and hunting threaten the species; protected areas and coordinated zoo breeding support conservation.

Diet

  • Wild: Leaves, fruits, seeds, nuts, roots, fungi, and occasionally small animals or invertebrates.

  • Human Care: A varied omnivore diet with browse, vegetables, fruit, and appropriate protein.

Reproduction

  • Nesting: After about five months of gestation, females usually give birth to one or two piglets.

  • Parental Care: The mother nurses and protects the piglets; litters are small compared with those of many pigs.

  • Development: Piglets begin sampling solid food early and are weaned over the following months.

Behavior & Key Traits

  • Key Trait: Males' upper tusks grow through the skin of the snout.

  • Behavior: Often active by day and rests during the hottest hours.

  • Adaptation: Females and young may form small groups while adult males are often solitary.

  • Ecological Role: A strong swimmer capable of crossing short sea channels.
