Malaysian Giant Pond Turtle

  • Common Names: Malaysian giant turtle, Bornean river turtle, Orlitia borneensis.

  • Status: Critically Endangered; wild populations are declining and poorly studied.

  • Range: The Malay Peninsula, Sumatra, and Borneo, with scattered records elsewhere in Southeast Asia.

  • Habitat: Large freshwater lakes, slow-flowing rivers, and peat-swamp forests.

  • Size & Weight: Usually 16-24 inches in shell length; exceptionally large turtles may exceed 3 feet and weigh up to about 110 lbs.

  • Lifespan: Estimated at roughly 80-100 years in suitable conditions.

Physical Description

  • Males: Males have longer, thicker tails than females and a smooth, dark shell.
  • Females: Females have shorter tails; both sexes have a dark oval shell and pale underside.

Predators & Conservation:

  • Predators: Crocodiles and other predators may take juveniles; people are the main threat to adults.
  • Conservation: Collection, trade, habitat change, and incidental capture threaten this poorly studied turtle; protection of rivers and trade controls are important.

Diet

  • Wild: Omnivorous, eating fish, aquatic invertebrates, aquatic plants, and fallen fruit.

  • Human Care: A varied aquatic-turtle diet with suitable animal protein and plant matter.

Reproduction

  • Nesting: Females lay a clutch of eggs on land or in riverbank debris; incubation takes roughly 90-120 days.

  • Parental Care: Adults do not provide extended care after the eggs are laid.

  • Development: Hatchlings have serrated shell edges and a pale line along the head and neck; the shell smooths as they grow.

Behavior & Key Traits

  • Key Trait: One of Southeast Asia's largest hard-shelled freshwater turtles.

  • Behavior: Spends much of its time in water and is secretive.

  • Adaptation: Has fully webbed feet and powerful jaws.

  • Ecological Role: Hatchlings remain vulnerable for several years as they grow.
