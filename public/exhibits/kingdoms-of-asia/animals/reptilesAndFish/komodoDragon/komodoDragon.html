Komodo Dragon

  • Common Names: Komodo monitor, ora, Varanus komodoensis.

  • Status: Endangered; climate change and human pressures threaten its restricted island habitat.

  • Range: Several Indonesian islands in the Lesser Sunda chain, including Komodo, Rinca, Flores, and Gili Motang.

  • Habitat: Tropical dry forest, savanna, open woodland, and coastal lowlands.

  • Size & Weight: Males commonly reach 8-10 feet and 150-200 lbs; females are generally smaller.

  • Lifespan: About 30-50 years in the wild.

Physical Description

  • Males: Males are typically larger and heavier than females, with a muscular body and powerful limbs.
  • Females: Females are generally smaller; both sexes have rough scales, strong claws, and a long forked tongue.

Predators & Conservation:

  • Predators: Adult dragons have few natural predators; eggs and juveniles may be eaten by larger dragons, raptors, pigs, or dogs.
  • Conservation: Protected habitat and monitoring are key; threats include prey poaching, human-wildlife conflict, and climate-driven habitat loss.

Diet

  • Wild: Juveniles eat insects, small reptiles, and birds; adults hunt or scavenge deer, pigs, and other large animals.

  • Human Care: A managed carnivore diet suited to age and size, with whole-prey items under veterinary care.

Reproduction

  • Nesting: Females lay roughly 15-30 eggs in a burrow or abandoned megapode nest; incubation lasts about 7-8 months.

  • Parental Care: There is no extended parental care; hatchlings climb trees to avoid adult cannibalism.

  • Development: Hatchlings are about 16 inches long and spend their early years in trees before becoming more terrestrial.

Behavior & Key Traits

  • Key Trait: The largest living lizard.

  • Behavior: Uses a specialized sensory organ to detect scent particles with its forked tongue.

  • Adaptation: Can swim between nearby islands.

  • Ecological Role: Has venom glands in the lower jaw and serrated teeth.
